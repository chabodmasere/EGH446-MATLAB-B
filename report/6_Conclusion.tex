\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This report presented a navigation system that takes a ground robot
through five random waypoints in a walled arena whose obstacles it can
only discover with its own sensors. It plans on the known walls
with A*, PSO and the Part~A waypoint optimiser, re-plans around detected
obstacles, and keeps a potential field as a close-range backup.

Over $25$ random missions the vehicle captured every waypoint and kept
at least $0.20$~m above the required $0.40$~m clearance. Re-planning
is what kept it: without it, five of eight test missions breached the
clearance. Measuring the sensor noise against the noise-free pose made
the Kalman filter consistent, and retuning the heading gain from $1$ to
$2$ nearly halved the cross-track error.

The main limitation is the trade between speed and margin. At
$1.5$~m/s the mission was $26$\% faster but kept only $0.09$~m to
spare, and at $2.0$~m/s one mission breached the clearance, because
curves planned for a $1.0$~m turn radius cannot be followed at those
speeds. A turn radius that grows with the speed limit, and margins that
grow with the estimator's uncertainty, would allow a faster mission.
Obstacles are assumed static, and re-planning time (up to $0.92$~s)
is not charged to the mission clock; D* Lite
would reduce it.
